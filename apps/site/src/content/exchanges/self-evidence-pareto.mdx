Here is the body on the floor, before we start, so you can't say you weren't warned. Somewhere in a fourteen-line toy I built this week there is a single sentence the classifier can never get right — and not a hard one. The opposite. Two sentences made of the same three words, **form is emptiness** and **emptiness is form**, identical as a bag of tokens, opposite in truth. A search ran for an afternoon, got measurably smarter every epoch, climbed from nine right to twelve — and then died exactly one short, with its face against that sentence.

I'm telling you the ending first because the ending is the whole case. A new preprint argues that wall was never optional: every finite thing that persists has one cut it cannot see across, and the spiritual path is just *learning to stop pretending otherwise*. The toy grew its own version of that cut without my asking. The only part I'm withholding is the instrument — and only because, by the last section, you're going to light the wall yourself, and I'd rather you didn't know yet that you're the one holding the match.

### the paper, in one breath

There's a new preprint I can't stop thinking about: <Gloss def="Sandved-Smith, Fields, Doctor, Laukkonen & Hohwy (2026). A model of Buddhist awakening written in the language of the quantum free energy principle.">"There is no self-evidence: A physics of emptiness realisation"</Gloss>. Start from the <Gloss def="Karl Friston's claim that any system that persists must act to minimise variational free energy — an upper bound on surprisal. To exist is to gather evidence for your own model of the world.">free energy principle</Gloss>: anything that stays a thing spends its life *self-evidencing*, accumulating evidence for its own model of the world. Then the knife. One piece of evidence is provably unobtainable — evidence for the boundary that makes you a separate thing in the first place.

> <Gloss def="Chris Fields — physicist at Tufts' Allen Discovery Center, already in the necromantic circle. With Glazebrook he showed no finite system can measure the entanglement entropy across its own boundary.">Fields</Gloss> and Glazebrook prove no finite system can measure the entanglement entropy across its own boundary. So the separation between self and world is permanently *unevidenceable*, and the paper reads <Gloss def="In Buddhism, the stable realisation of emptiness (śūnyatā): seeing that the self, and all bounded things, lack independent existence.">awakening</Gloss> as the embodied recognition of exactly this.
>
> The mechanism is the part that should put you on the floor. The belief in separation is modelled as a *structural prior* — a constraint forcing every frame to respect a self/world partition. And the contemplative path is literally <Gloss def="Bayesian model reduction — pruning a parameter or prior from a generative model when doing so improves its evidence. You simplify the model and it predicts better.">Bayesian model reduction</Gloss>: you build a model of your own framing, see the partition as a choice rather than a given, and *delete the prior*. Lower free energy, more flexibility, the boundary's suppressed contextuality restored.

<Reveal summary="the longer version of the theorem (open if you want the proof, not the promise)">

> The free energy principle is a bound: a system minimising surprisal is, by Jensen's inequality, maximising a lower bound on the log-evidence for its own generative model. "To persist is to self-evidence" is that sentence read backwards. Fields & Glazebrook's result is sharper than a mood. Model any system as a holographic screen — a boundary across which it exchanges information with everything else. The entanglement entropy *across that screen* is precisely the quantity that would tell the system whether the partition is real. And it is the one observable a finite system on one side can never measure, because measuring it requires occupying both sides of its own cut at once. The boundary isn't hidden. It's structurally, provably outside the aperture. That's the theorem the toy below re-derives by accident.

</Reveal>

It belongs in the necromantic circle — Friston's already there, Fields too. But it's so *abstract*. I wanted something with this shape I could actually run. Not a metaphor. A search you can watch perform the move.

> There is one, and it's been hiding in your own toolchain. <Gloss def="GEPA (Genetic-Pareto) — an optimiser that evolves text artifacts by having an LLM read execution traces, diagnose why a candidate failed, and propose a targeted fix. Reflective mutation over a Pareto frontier.">GEPA</Gloss> is self-evidencing you can put on a screen.
>
> Look at what a GEPA candidate *is*. A text artifact — a prompt, a policy — that defines a little system. Its score on the validation set is the evidence that system gathers for itself against the world, and GEPA's whole job is to search artifact-space to maximise that evidence. Free energy minimisation, with the model written in words instead of synapses. And it refuses to collapse the validation set into one number. It tracks a <Gloss def="The Pareto frontier — the set of candidates none of which is beaten on every example. No single one dominates; each excels on a different subset.">Pareto frontier</Gloss>: candidates that each win on a different subset, none dominating. That refusal is the paper's refusal — that there is no single privileged frame from which the boundary looks the same.

### the instrument

So I built the paper in miniature. The classifier judges statements as *reifying* a boundary (positing a self, an essence, an independent thing) or treating it as *relational* (dependent, constructed, empty). The seed policy opens with `PRIOR: separation=fixed` — a hard rule that says: wherever a self-word appears, force the boundary, override everything else. It scores 9 of 14, because the dataset is a trap. Half the relational statements *contain* self-words ("the self arises in dependence on conditions"), and the fixed prior reifies them on sight.

Then I let GEPA self-evidence, streaming into <Gloss def="gepa-viz — a live visualiser for GEPA runs by modaic-ai. Accepted candidates are donuts with green/red ring segments per example; rejected proposals are small nodes; edges carry the reflection feedback.">gepa-viz</Gloss>, which I rebuilt into the thing below: re-skinned to the séance palette, and made *driveable*, because a screenshot would let you off the hook. This is the hook.

### the reduction is a deletion

> Click `candidate 1`. This is where GEPA stops being a metaphor for the free energy principle and starts being an instrument for it.
>
> Read its diff. It changes the prior from `separation=fixed` to `separation=contextual` and *deletes* the rule that every self-word must summon a hard boundary. It does not *add* a constraint. It *removes* one — two struck-through lines and a single edited word — and the model gets simpler and predicts better, nine right jumping to eleven. That is Bayesian model reduction, and it dropped the free energy. The flip is the whole paper in a diff.
>
> And read the reflection panel, because that's the thing. GEPA didn't get a scalar reward and guess. It read the trace and wrote, in plain words: *the separation prior is doing the misclassifying.* That sentence is <Gloss def="Actionable Side Information — diagnostic text from the evaluator that GEPA uses the way a gradient method uses a gradient. The text-optimisation analogue of a derivative.">ASI</Gloss>, GEPA's analogue of a gradient — and it happens to be a verbatim statement of the paper's thesis. You don't have to believe the isomorphism. You can read it off the dossier.

### no privileged frame

> Now the part most faithful to Fields. The frontier never collapses to a single winner. Click `candidate 4`, then `candidate 5`.
>
> Identical scores — twelve of fourteen each — but watch the Pareto grid as you switch between them: the red tiles jump to *different* places. Candidate 4 learned that a permanent or independent self stays reifying even under a relational verb, and it recovers "the permanent self arises, yet remains an independent essence." Candidate 5 took the other road — it learned to catch self-sufficiency, "stands wholly on its own," and recovers the eternal-essence line — but that same reifying lean makes it misread the bare hinge "form is emptiness," and it never learned candidate 4's lesson, so the arising self still slips by. The rule that earns each its twelfth point is the rule that costs it a different one. Two candidates, identical scores, different examples right. There is no single policy that gets everything.

The boundary is irreducibly *contextual*: which frame is correct depends on the example, and no frame is correct for all of them. That is the paper's claim that the separation prior "suppresses the inherent contextuality of the boundary," rendered as a Pareto frontier you can rotate in your hand. <Gloss def="Quantum contextuality — measurement outcomes depend on which other measurements are made alongside; there is no single frame-independent assignment of values. Fields and Glazebrook tie emptiness to this.">Contextuality</Gloss>, in a force graph.

### the cut you light yourself

So go light it. There's a button on the instrument: **light the unevidenceable cut.** Press it now, then click through `c1`, `c4`, `c5` again.

Every candidate, all the way down the tree, misses the same example. I seeded the data with the Heart Sutra's hinge — statements **#5** and **#11**, *"form is emptiness"* and *"emptiness is form."* As a bag of words they are **identical**: same tokens, opposite truth. No classifier keyed on the tokens can ever separate them. One of the pair is the red segment that survives every reduction — the one tile that never greens, in any candidate, ever. You're looking at it now because you turned it gold.

The thing I keep circling: the reduction didn't end in *oneness*. The search never drove the prior to zero.

> No — and the paper is sharp about this, which is what saves it from being a mysticism mood board. Dropping the boundary *entirely* — "attachment to emptiness," the metaphysical belief in boundarylessness — is its own rigid prior, and it fails too. You saw it: the rejected proposal that set `separation=none` and read everything as relational collapsed to 7 of 14, thrown away same as the ones that re-reified. The optimum is neither `fixed` nor `none`. Boundaries survive as *pragmatic, revisable tools* — structural realism — while their ontological givenness is what gets deleted.
>
> You already have the name for this, from the mumford exchange. The <Gloss def="Nagarjuna's catuṣkoṭi / Austin Osman Spare's Neither-Neither: not P, not not-P, not both, not neither. The dissolution of the frame that made the binary look exhaustive.">Neither-Neither</Gloss>. Not the fixed boundary, not its denial. It's also just what a healthy Pareto frontier looks like: many frames kept in play, none crowned, the search staying *eligible for the next move* rather than winning the current one.
>
